Features

Small app. Big time saver.

Stop re-copying the same things. Clips quietly remembers, so you don't have to.

Instant search

Type a few letters to find that link, address or snippet you copied days ago.

Pin & name favorites

Keep your email, IBAN or signature on top with ⌘P, and give them names like “My IBAN” with ⌘R.

Paste in a keystroke

⌘1 to ⌘9 paste your latest items instantly, right where your cursor is.

Text, images & files

Screenshots, photos and Finder files are saved too, with a live preview. Copy a picture in Finder and paste it straight into a chat.

Plain-text paste

Messy formatting from the web? ⇧↩ pastes clean, plain text.

Private by design

Everything stays on your Mac, encrypted. No account, no cloud, no tracking. Passwords are skipped and API keys are hidden.

Encrypted history. Hidden secrets.

Get started

Install in a minute

Two ways to install. Pick whichever you like.

1Download & drag

Download Clips.dmg, open it, and drag Clips onto the Applications folder.

→

2Open Clips, then click Done

Open Clips from Applications. The first time, macOS shows a warning saying it can't verify the app. That's expected: just click Done.

⚠️
“Clips” Not Opened

Apple could not verify “Clips” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.

Done

3Click “Open Anyway”

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll to the bottom. Next to “Clips” was blocked, click Open Anyway, then enter your Mac password.

You only need to do this once.

4Allow pasting, press ⇧⌘V

When Clips asks, turn it on in Privacy & Security → Accessibility so it can paste for you. Then press ⇧⌘V anywhere. Done!

Why the warning? Apple only skips it for apps from developers in its paid developer program. Clips isn't part of it yet, so macOS asks you to confirm once. Clips never sends your clipboard anywhere. Prefer to skip the warning? Install with Terminal instead.

Paste this into Terminal

Open Terminal (press ⌘Space, type “Terminal”), paste the line below and press Return. No warning, no dragging.

curl -fsSL https://www.clipsapp.online/install.sh | bash
  1. Downloads the latest Clips from this website
  2. Installs it in your Applications folder
  3. Opens it: allow Accessibility when asked, then press ⇧⌘V
Curious what it does? Read the install script. Run the same line again any time to reinstall.
FAQ

Questions

Everything you might want to know.

Is Clips free?

Yes, Clips is free to download and use.

Can I give names to saved items?

Yes. Select an item and press ⌘R (or right-click → Rename…), type a name like “My IBAN” and press Return. Named items are pinned, show their name in bold, and you can search by the name. To remove a name, rename it to nothing.

Can I see only images, links or files?

Yes. Open Clips, press Tab (or click “All Types” next to the search), pick a type with the arrow keys and press Return. You can show text, images, files, links, emails or colors, and search within that type. Choose “All Types” to see everything again.

Does Clips send my data anywhere?

No. Your history is stored only on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/Clips. Your clipboard history is never sent anywhere; Clips only goes online to check for updates.

Is my history encrypted?

Yes, since version 1.1.4. Your history and saved images are encrypted with AES-256, and the key is kept in your Mac's Keychain. Other apps, scripts and backups only see scrambled data. History from earlier versions is converted automatically when you update.

What happens to API keys and tokens I copy?

Clips recognizes API keys (OpenAI, Anthropic, GitHub, AWS, Stripe, Slack, Google and more), tokens, JWTs, HMAC secrets, private keys and lines like API_KEY=…. They're shown hidden (hold ⌥ to see one), pasted with a marker that tells other clipboard apps not to save them, and deleted after 24 hours. Pin one if you want to keep it.

Does it record passwords?

Clips skips anything that password managers (1Password, Bitwarden, Keychain and others) mark as private. You can also pause recording from the menu bar at any time.

Why does it need Accessibility permission?

Only to paste the item you pick into the app you're using (it presses ⌘V for you). Without it, Clips still works: picking an item copies it and you press ⌘V yourself.

Can I change the shortcut?

Yes. Click the paperclip in the menu bar → Shortcut, and choose ⇧⌘V, ⌥⌘V, ⌃⌥V or ⇧⌘C.

How many items does it keep?

The last 300 items, plus everything you pin. Pinned items are never removed automatically.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit Clips from the menu bar and move it from Applications to the Trash. To also remove your history, delete ~/Library/Application Support/Clips.
